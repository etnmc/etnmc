    01

    Buttons and modes

    There are four buttons. C moves through five modes in a fixed loop. Holding A for two seconds opens the setting screen of the mode that is showing. Stopwatch is the exception: it has nothing to set.

    Mode loop

    C ▸ C ▸ C ▸ C ▸

    The upper line is what the top left of the display shows in that mode. C from Timer goes back to Timekeeping. Hold C for one second in any mode to go straight there. Select a mode here to jump the simulator to it.

    What each button is for

    ButtonNormal screensSetting screens
    A upper leftHold 2 s: open settings. Alarm: on or off. Stopwatch: split and reset. Timer: reset when paused.Save and exit
    B upper rightLight. Hold 3 s in Timekeeping: auto light on or off.Value down
    C lower leftNext mode. Hold 1 s: Timekeeping.Next item
    D lower rightStart and stop, next item, last sync result. Hold 2 s in Timekeeping: receive now.Value up, or switch

    Reading the display

    1. Top left Day of the week in Timekeeping. WT, AL, ST or TR in the other modes. HT while you set the Home City and RC during a reception.
    2. Window The date in Timekeeping. Home time in World Time. The current time in Stopwatch and Timer. Names and values in setting screens.
    3. Main digits The time for the mode on screen: time of day, alarm time, elapsed time or countdown.
    4. Small digits Seconds. Hundredths in the stopwatch. The word on, or two dashes, in Alarm. L1 to L3 during a reception.
    5. Bottom bars SNZ, ALM and SIG on the left. One charge bar above L, M or H on the right.

    Select an indicator to make it blink on the watch.

    • ManualSetting screens close on their own after two to three minutes without a press. Alarm mode also drops back to Timekeeping after that time.
    • ManualPS means power saving is enabled. It does not mean the watch is asleep.
    • ReportIn Stopwatch and Timer the top-right window shows the current time. The older module 3159 did not do this.
    02

    Radio time sync

    Each night the watch listens for a long-wave time signal and corrects itself. With London as Home City it uses MSF from Cumbria or DCF77 from Germany and picks between them by itself.

      The highlighted row is the one your current Home City listens for. The 3495 guide only marks which cities can receive. The city groups for JJY and BPC follow Casio's documentation for earlier Multi Band 6 modules.

      • ManualAutomatic reception runs between midnight and 5 a.m. After one success the watch makes no more attempts that day.
      • CasioUp to six attempts a night, five for the Chinese signal.
      • ManualOne reception normally takes three to eight minutes and can take sixteen. Leave the watch near a window with its 12 o'clock side toward the glass. Keep it still and away from metal, appliances and phones.
      • ManualNothing is received when the charge is low, in Alarm, Stopwatch or Timer mode, during a countdown, at power saving level 2, or when the Home City has no signal. An alarm that sounds during a reception ends it.
      • ManualLevel 1 power saving, where only the display is off, is not on that list. So the watch can still sync while it sleeps at level 1.
      • ReportRCVD means the watch has synced since midnight. It goes out at the first attempt of the new day, after a failed manual receive, and after a manual change to the time, date or Home City. RCVD being off in the evening is not a fault.
      • ManualPress D in Timekeeping to see the date and time of the last successful sync. This is the dependable check.
      • ManualDuring a manual receive the seconds position shows L1, L2 or L3. L3 is a stable signal. It takes about ten seconds to settle, so use it to find a good spot.
      • ReportMSF is taken off air for planned maintenance several times a year, mostly in daytime. Overnight syncs are rarely affected, and DCF77 is the fallback.
      • SimA receive takes nine seconds here. ERR after a failed receive is how earlier modules show it. The 3495 guide does not show that screen.
      03

      Solar power

      The solar panel is built into the face around the display. It charges a small rechargeable cell. The bar above L, M or H shows the charge.

      Charge levels

      Select a level to put the simulator in that state.

      Light needed for one day of running

      Outdoor sun50,000 lx8 min
      Sunny, by a window10,000 lx30 min
      Overcast, by a window5,000 lx48 min
      Indoor fluorescent500 lx8 h

      Bars are to scale. Indoor light alone barely keeps up.

      Hours of light to recover

      A dash means the manual gives no figure. Indoor light cannot get it there.

      Power saving

      about 1 h in the dark between 22:00 and 06:00 ▸ 6 to 7 days like that ▸

      To wake it from either level, press any button, move it into bright light, or tilt it toward you with auto light on. It never sleeps in Stopwatch or Timer mode, or with power saving switched off in settings.

      • ManualFrom a full charge the watch runs about 10 months with no light at all. That assumes one 1.5 s light use, one alarm and four minutes of reception a day, plus six hours a day in power saving.
      • CasioAbout 22 months if it is stored in the dark with power saving on.
      • ManualH, M and L flashing together is charge recovery. It follows several power-hungry actions close together, such as light, alarm and reception. Functions are limited for about 15 minutes, less in bright light.
      • ManualDo not charge it on a car dashboard, close to an incandescent bulb, or anywhere it gets hot. The display can turn black when hot and recovers as it cools.
      • ManualA sleeve over the face cuts charging and can send the watch into power saving while you wear it.
      • ManualThe cell is not meant to be changed by the owner. Its capacity fades over years of use.
      • UnverifiedParts listings show a CTL1616-type lithium cell (2.3 V) for Casio's solar squares. This is not confirmed for module 3495.
      • SimAfter a flat battery the simulator restarts at 1 January 2021 with Tokyo as Home City. The real factory defaults are not documented.
      04

      Light

      B lights the display with a white LED for 1.5 or 3 seconds. Auto light does the same when you tilt the watch toward your face in the dark.

      Surroundings

      Auto light needs all of these

        Set the surroundings to dark, switch auto light on, then drag the angle past 40°.

        • CasioWhite LED with afterglow. It stays on for 1.5 s or 3 s per press. Choose which in settings.
        • ReportThe LED sits at the left edge of the display, so the left side is brighter than the right.
        • ManualInside setting screens B changes the value and does not light the display.
        • ReportAuto light only fires in the dark. The solar panel doubles as the light sensor, so there is no separate sensor to cover or clean.
        • ManualAuto light can misfire under a sleeve and run the battery down. It can also fail if your forearm is more than 15° away from horizontal when you tilt the watch.
        • ManualThere is no light while a tone is sounding, during reception, or at charge level L and below.
        • ManualA faint rattle when you shake the watch is the tilt switch. It is normal.
        • ReportGuides written for older squares call the auto light indicator A.EL. On this module it reads LT.
        • UnverifiedOwners of earlier solar squares report that auto light stays on until you switch it off, while non-solar models cancel it after about six hours. This is not confirmed for module 3495.
        05

        Settings

        In Timekeeping, hold A for two seconds. SET and Hold appear, then HT flashes and the Home City name scrolls. Each press of C moves to the next of 15 items. D changes the value, B lowers it where a value can go down, and A saves and exits.

        The values on the right are live from the simulator. Open an item for detail, or let the demo take you to it.

          06

          World time

          World Time holds five cities. The large digits show the selected slot and the window at the top right shows your Home time. D steps through the slots.

          Your five slots

          • Manual48 cities in 31 time zones, plus UTC.
          • ManualSummer time is set by hand for each slot, OFF or ON. There is no AUTO here, so check your slots after the clocks change. UTC has no summer-time option.
          • ManualPressing A and B together swaps the Home City with the slot on screen. After a swap the watch listens for the new Home City's transmitter. If that city has none, radio sync stops until you swap back.
          • ReportKathmandu, Buenos Aires and Fernando de Noronha are new on this module. Miami, Toronto and Santiago were dropped.
          City table · 49 entries

          Listed in the order the watch steps through them with D. Selecting a city here is a simulator shortcut. On the watch, hold A and use B and D.

          07

          Alarm, stopwatch, timer

          Alarm · AL

          1. D steps through -1-, -2-, -3-, -4-, SNZ and SIG.
          2. A switches the item on screen on or off. The seconds position reads on, or two dashes for off.
          3. Hold A for 2 s to set the time. Hours flash. C moves to minutes, B and D change the value, A saves.
          • ManualFour daily alarms and one snooze alarm. There are no weekday or date options.
          • ManualThe tone lasts 10 seconds. Any button stops it.
          • ManualSnooze sounds up to seven times at five-minute gaps. To cancel it, switch SNZ off.
          • ManualAlarms are silent at low charge and at power saving level 2. MUTE does not silence them.
          • ReportOwners describe the tone as quiet. Do not rely on it to wake you through bedding.
          • SimThe simulator switches an alarm on when you enter its time. On the watch, check that the seconds position reads on.

          Timer · TR

          Running itD start▸D pause▸D resume▸tone 10 s
          1. With the start time showing, hold A for 2 s. Hours flash.
          2. C moves from hours to minutes to seconds. B and D change the value. A saves.
          3. A resets a paused countdown to the start time.
          • ManualStart time from 1 second to 24 hours in one-second steps. A start time of 0:00 00 gives 24 hours.

          • ManualA running countdown blocks radio sync. The watch does not enter power saving in Timer mode.
          • ReportSetting seconds is new. Module 3159 could only set minutes and hours.
          08

          Care and limits

          Water · 20 bar

          Swimming, windsurfing and skin diving are fine. It is not for scuba with air tanks. Do not press buttons under water or while it is wet. Avoid hot baths, saunas and soap. Rinse it in fresh water after the sea.

          Resin strap and bezel

          Sweat and water left on the resin cause early wear, cracks and breaks. Rinse and dry it after heavy use. Keep solvents, oils, fuel, adhesives and cosmetics off it. Replace the strap when it stiffens, cracks or changes colour.

          Seals and service

          Casio advises new gaskets every two to three years to keep the water rating. Battery work should go to a Casio service centre, which checks water resistance afterwards.

          Temperature

          The display is hard to read below 0 °C and above 40 °C. Long periods above 60 °C damage it. Timekeeping drifts at both extremes.

          Buttons

          Press each button now and then. Buttons that are never used can become stiff.

          Clock limits

          The calendar runs from 2000 to 2099. Without a radio sync the watch keeps within 15 seconds a month.

          Static and magnets

          Strong static can blank the display for a moment. Strong static or magnetism can also upset the auto light tilt switch. Lower your arm and tilt again.

          All from the Operation Guide.
